# Go-to-Market Strategy

### Who is the exact target user?
We are not targeting "startups" broadly. We are specifically targeting **Fractional CFOs** and **VP of Engineering** at Series A / Series B startups (20–100 employees). 
At this stage, the company is big enough to have serious SaaS bloat, but small enough that no one is actively managing procurement. The VP Eng is annoyed by the $3k/mo ChatGPT Enterprise bill, and the Fractional CFO is looking for quick wins to show the founders they are saving runway.

### What they Google right before they need this
- "How to track employee ChatGPT subscriptions"
- "Cursor business vs enterprise plan differences"
- "SaaS spend management tools for startups"
- "Rippling software spend report"

### Where they hang out online
- **Fractional CFOs:** Niche LinkedIn groups, specifically around "SaaS metrics" or "Startup Finance Operations". They don't hang out on Hacker News.
- **VPs of Engineering:** Private Slack communities like *Rands in Repose* or *EngL (Engineering Leadership)*. Specifically the `#vendor-tools` or `#budget` channels.

### How to get the first 100 users in 30 days ($0 budget)
I wouldn't waste time on generic Twitter posts.
1. **Cold Email Fractional CFOs:** I'd pull a list of 500 fractional CFOs from Apollo.io. The email pitch is incredibly simple: *"Hey [Name], I built a free calculator that instantly audits your client's AI spend. Usually finds ~$500/mo in overlaps (Copilot + Cursor double billing). Run your biggest client through it here: [link]."*
2. **"Drop-in" the Rands Slack:** I'd wait for someone in an engineering leadership Slack to complain about OpenAI pricing, and then casually drop the link: *"Yeah it's getting out of hand. I built this quick audit tool last weekend to see how bad our overlap was, might be useful for your team."*
3. **Product Hunt Launch:** Position it as the "Mint for AI Tools". Clean branding, heavy emphasis on the fact that it doesn't require connecting your bank account or OAuth to use.

### The Unfair Distribution Channel
Credex's unfair advantage is that they *actually have the discounted credits*. 
Other SaaS management tools just say "you are overspending." AuditAI says "You are overspending, and here is a button to immediately buy the exact same software 20% cheaper." It's an instant-gratification loop that traditional SaaS management platforms (like Vendr or Tropic) can't offer without long sales cycles.

### What week-1 traction looks like
If the launch works, week 1 should look like this:
- 5,000 unique visitors (mostly from Product Hunt and Hacker News)
- 800 audits completed (16% conversion to usage)
- 300 emails captured (37% of completions)
- 15 consultations booked for Credex (5% of leads)
- At least 5 "Shareable Reports" posted organically on X by founders flexing their highly optimized stacks.
